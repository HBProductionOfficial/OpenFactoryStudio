Emulator

The Emulator runs your project inside the program, in an engine that follows M.U.G.E.N's rules. It needs a M.U.G.E.N engine folder for the common states, the fight screen, fonts and stages. It uses the folder your project is in, or the one you type in Engine directory on the panel. It runs what is in the editors right now, unsaved changes included.

What it plays

  • Characters fight a copy of themselves (Enemy: Self) or a random character from the engine folder. The fight is on the engine's first stage, with the engine folder's fight screen, sounds and announcer, and the stage's music.
  • Stages and fight screens are shown with the engine's character.
  • Storyboards play their scenes with their music.
  • System screens (motifs): pick a screen with Screen on the panel. It can be the title menu, versus, continue, victory, win or survival results screen. The title, versus and victory screens play their music.

Music files (WAV, OGG and MP3) are loaded from the engine folder. MIDI music doesn't play.

Playing

Click the workspace and play with the player 1 and player 2 keys set in Options > MUGENext. The mouse wheel zooms in and out. View > Attack box and Collision box draw the contact boxes on top of the game. On the panel you set the mode (Versus or Training), the rounds, the round time and the explod rules (M.U.G.E.N 1.0, 1.1, or each character's own version). You can also choose whether debug text is drawn under the players.

Training

In Training mode, life and power come back and nobody gets knocked out. There is no time limit. The dummy (player 2) can be Cooperative: it stands, crouches or jumps, guards by itself, keeps a distance and mashes a button, depending on your settings. It can also be AI, which is the computer at the level you pick, or Manual, which uses player 2's keys. Your changes take effect while the match is running.

Debugging

Every match is also a debug session (Debug > Start emulator).

  • Breakpoints on [State] lines and trigger lines in the code files stop the match when a character gets to them. A breakpoint can have a condition (a M.U.G.E.N expression) and an ignore count, and it can be one-shot. Frame breakpoints stop before a frame. Data breakpoints stop after a value changes.
  • Interrupt stops before the next frame. Step into goes to the next line, and Step over goes to the next line of the same character. Step out leaves the current state. Step frame goes to the next frame. Continue keeps the match running.
  • When the match stops, the place where it stopped opens in its editor with the current line marked. The Debug panel shows the values of the match and of every character (life, power, position, states, variables and so on). Right-click a value to watch it or to add a data breakpoint. The Debugger panel shows the call stack and the breakpoints. The Watch panel shows the values you are watching.
